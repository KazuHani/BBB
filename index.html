Big Changes Planned for the Country

The "One Big Beautiful Bill Act" (OBBBA) suggests major shifts in how the government handles money, rules, and services. We'll break down the main ideas: careful government spending, fewer rules for businesses, stronger law enforcement, and big changes to social programs and taxes. See how these changes could affect money and rules in America.

💰

Money & Spending

More money for things like border security, but big cuts for areas like climate change and environmental protection. Plus, many new fees for government services.

⚖️

Fewer Rules

Getting rid of many environmental rules and less government watch over banks and financial companies. Congress would get more say over rules than government agencies.

🛡️

Stronger Enforcement

A very big focus on immigration laws, with billions of dollars for holding facilities, more staff, and building a border wall.

📝

Taxes & Social Programs

Keeping the 2017 tax cuts, getting rid of tax breaks for green energy, and adding new work rules and limits for government support programs (like food stamps or welfare).

Big Changes to Immigration & Border Security

This bill plans to spend over $125 billion to change how immigration and border security work. This means a lot of money for a border wall, much more space to hold people, more border officers, and many new fees for almost every immigration step that you can't get out of paying.

New Money for Border Control and Facilities

The bill gives tens of billions of dollars to specific government groups for border control. Most of this money is for building the border wall and for ICE (Immigration and Customs Enforcement) to hold more people. Hover over the bars to see how much money each part gets.

Changes to How We Pay Taxes

This bill makes the 2017 tax cuts for people and businesses permanent. It also adds some new, specific tax breaks and gets rid of most tax credits for clean energy (like solar or wind). Click on any card to learn more about these changes.

📉

2017 Tax Cuts Made Permanent

Keeps the current income tax rates for individuals, the higher standard amount you can deduct from your taxes, and the $2,000 Child Tax Credit.

  • Tax Rates for Individuals: The current tax rate levels (10%, 12%, 22%, etc.) will stay.
  • Standard Deduction: The larger amount everyone can subtract from their income before paying taxes will stay and get a temporary boost.
  • Child Tax Credit: The $2,000 credit for each child will stay, and temporarily go up to $2,500 until 2028.
  • Business Income Deduction (199A): The 20% tax break for certain business owners will stay and increase to 23%.
  • Estate Tax (Inheritance Tax): The amount you can inherit before paying taxes will stay high, starting at $15 million.
🎉

New Specific Tax Breaks

Adds new ways to lower your taxes, like for money earned from tips and overtime pay, plus new types of savings accounts.

  • No Tax on Tips: A new way for workers who get tips (like waiters) to lower their taxes on that tip money.
  • No Tax on Overtime: A new way to lower taxes on money earned from working overtime.
  • Car Loan Interest: For a limited time, you can lower your taxes by the amount of interest paid on car loans for cars made in the U.S.
  • MAGA Accounts: New savings accounts called "Money Account for Growth and Advancement" for people under 18. Money in these accounts won't be taxed, and they start with a $1,000 government contribution.
🍃

Green Energy Tax Breaks Removed

Stops or slowly removes almost all major tax breaks for clean energy (like solar panels or electric cars) for both people and companies.

  • Clean Cars: Tax breaks for buying new, used, or business-owned clean energy cars will end.
  • Home Energy: Tax breaks for making your home more energy-efficient or installing things like solar panels will end.
  • Clean Electricity: Tax breaks for making or investing in clean electricity will be slowly removed and will have new rules if foreign companies are involved.
  • Advanced Manufacturing: The tax break for making parts for clean energy systems will be slowly removed and have new limits.

Changes to Healthcare and Support Programs

This bill makes big changes to programs like Medicaid (health insurance for low-income people), ACA (Affordable Care Act or "Obamacare"), SNAP (food stamps), and student loans. The main ideas are making it harder to qualify, requiring more work or community service, better checks to prevent fraud, and adding specific rules based on social issues.

Main Changes to Medicaid & Food Stamps (SNAP)

  • ✓
    Work/Community Service Rules: States must make certain people on Medicaid work, do community service, or go to school for 80 hours a month to keep their benefits.
  • ✓
    Tougher Food Stamp Rules: Stops automatic increases to food stamp amounts (which limits how much people get), makes people work until age 65 to get benefits, and the federal government will pay less for running the program.
  • ✓
    Better Fraud Prevention: New national systems to check addresses and stop people from getting Medicaid in more than one state. Stricter checks for citizenship and income to qualify.
  • ✗
    Social Issue Limits: Stops federal money from paying for gender transition treatments for young people on Medicaid/CHIP. Also, some places that offer abortions will no longer get Medicaid funding.

Changes to Education & "Obamacare" (ACA)

  • ➔
    New Student Loan Plan: Most people with student loans will have to use a new single "Repayment Assistance Plan." Other plans based on income will go away, and new loans won't allow pausing payments due to financial problems or job loss.
  • ➔
    Job Training Grants (Pell): Creates a new "Workforce Pell Grant" for short job training programs that must show good results. It will be harder for part-time students to get regular Pell Grants.
  • ➔
    Stricter "Obamacare" Sign-ups: Less time to sign up for health insurance through the ACA, and stricter checks on income and who qualifies for help paying for it.
  • ✗
    Limit on Covered Health Services: Gender transition treatments will not be considered an "Essential Health Benefit," meaning ACA health plans won't have to cover them.

Big Shifts in Energy and Environmental Rules

This bill makes a big change in U.S. energy plans. It moves away from supporting clean energy (like solar and wind) and focuses on getting more oil, gas, and coal from inside the country. It requires new sales of land for drilling and mining, and it cancels funding and rules meant to fight climate change and protect the environment.

Money Cut from Climate and Environment Programs

The bill takes back billions of dollars that were set aside for programs from the Inflation Reduction Act and other environmental laws but haven't been spent yet. The chart below shows some of the main areas where money is being cut.

Rules Favoring Fossil Fuels

  • ▶
    Required Land Sales: Forces the government to immediately start selling rights to drill for oil and gas on land in 9+ states every three months, and also requires new sales for offshore drilling.
  • ▶
    Alaska Drilling: Brings back canceled drilling rights and forces new sales of land for drilling in specific areas of Alaska.
  • ▶
    More Coal Mining: Requires faster approval for coal mining rights and makes an extra 4 million acres of land available for coal mining.
  • ▶
    Faster Approvals: Creates a new, quicker way for oil and gas drilling projects to get approved, called "permit-by-rule."

Environmental Rules Removed

  • ▶
    Clean Air Act Changes: Gets rid of the fund to reduce greenhouse gases, money for clean trucks and buses, and programs to cut pollution at shipping ports.
  • ▶
    Car Pollution Rules: Removes EPA rules about how much pollution new cars can release and NHTSA rules about how good their gas mileage must be.
  • ▶
    Methane Program Ended: Cancels and takes back all money for the program that encouraged oil and gas companies to reduce methane leaks.
  • ▶
    New Fees for Renewables: Adds new fees for using federal land for wind and solar power projects, based on land area and power produced.

Changing How Government Works & for Federal Employees

This bill greatly changes the power balance between Congress (lawmakers) and government agencies (like the EPA or FDA). It also changes what it's like to work for the federal government. It makes it harder for agencies to create new rules and changes retirement plans and job security for government workers.

Congress Gets More Say Over Rules

New steps are set up so Congress has to directly approve important rules made by government agencies. This will likely make it take longer to create new rules.

Agency Suggests a Rule

Agencies will have to give very detailed reports on costs, effects on jobs, and how it might affect prices.

NEW: Congress Must Approve

Any "major rule" that would bring in more money for the government can't start unless Congress officially agrees to it.

NEW: Yearly Review by Congress

Each year, agencies must ask Congress to re-approve 20% of their existing rules. If Congress doesn't approve them again, the rules will end.

Rule Stopped or Ends

If Congress doesn't approve a rule, it can't be put into place or continued.

Big Changes for Federal Government Jobs

The bill changes the retirement plan for federal workers and introduces "at-will" employment (meaning easier to fire). This creates two different systems for new government employees.

  • ▲
    Higher Retirement Payments: Most federal workers will have to pay more into their retirement plan over time, which means less take-home pay.
  • ▼
    "High-5" Pension Math: Pensions will be based on the average of the highest 5 years of salary, instead of the highest 3. This usually means a smaller pension.
  • ↔️
    "At-Will" Job Option: New hires can choose to be "at-will" employees (meaning they can be fired without the usual appeal process). If they choose this, they won't have to pay the higher retirement amounts. This is a big change to job protections for government workers.
