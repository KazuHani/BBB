A Sweeping Legislative Overhaul

The "One Big Beautiful Bill Act" proposes a fundamental reorientation of federal policy. This interactive analysis breaks down its core pillars: fiscal austerity, deregulation, enhanced enforcement, and significant shifts in social and tax policy. Explore the key financial and regulatory changes reshaping America's landscape.

💰

Fiscal Reshaping

Massive spending increases in some areas (border security) and deep cuts in others (climate, environment), alongside a proliferation of new user fees.

⚖️

Regulatory Rollback

Systematic dismantling of environmental regulations and reduced oversight for the financial services industry, shifting power from agencies to Congress.

🛡️

Enforcement First

An unprecedented focus on immigration enforcement, with tens of billions allocated for detention, personnel, and a physical border barrier.

📝

Tax & Social Policy

Making the 2017 tax cuts permanent while eliminating green energy credits and introducing new work requirements and restrictions in social safety net programs.

Immigration & Border Security Overhaul

The bill allocates over $125 billion to reshape immigration enforcement and border security. This includes massive funding for a border wall, a dramatic increase in detention capacity and enforcement personnel, and a host of new, non-waivable fees for nearly every step of the immigration process.

New Appropriations for Enforcement & Infrastructure

Taxation Overhaul

The bill makes permanent the individual and business tax cuts from the 2017 Tax Cuts and Jobs Act, while simultaneously introducing new, targeted tax relief measures and eliminating most clean energy tax credits. Click on any card to see more details about the proposed changes.

📉

TCJA Tax Cuts Permanent

Extends individual income tax rates, the increased standard deduction, and the $2,000 Child Tax Credit.

  • Individual Rates: The 10%, 12%, 22%, 24%, 32%, 35%, and 37% brackets are made permanent.
  • Standard Deduction: The higher standard deduction amounts are made permanent and temporarily enhanced.
  • Child Tax Credit: The $2,000 credit is made permanent, with a temporary boost to $2,500 through 2028.
  • Business Income Deduction (199A): The 20% pass-through deduction is made permanent and increased to 23%.
  • Estate Tax Exemption: The increased exemption is made permanent and set at a $15 million base.
🎉

New Targeted Relief

Introduces new tax deductions for income from tips and overtime, plus new savings accounts.

  • No Tax on Tips: Creates a new deduction for qualified tip income for workers in traditionally tipped occupations.
  • No Tax on Overtime: Creates a new deduction for qualified overtime compensation.
  • Car Loan Interest: Allows a temporary deduction for interest paid on loans for U.S.-assembled passenger vehicles.
  • MAGA Accounts: Establishes new tax-exempt "Money Account for Growth and Advancement" for individuals under 18, seeded with a $1,000 pilot credit.
🍃

Green Credits Eliminated

Terminates or phases out nearly all major clean energy tax credits for individuals and businesses.

  • Clean Vehicles: Credits for new, used, and commercial clean vehicles are terminated.
  • Residential Energy: Credits for home energy improvements and residential clean energy (like solar panels) are terminated.
  • Clean Electricity: Production and investment credits for clean electricity are phased out and hit with new restrictions related to foreign entities.
  • Advanced Manufacturing: The credit for producing clean energy components is phased out and restricted.

Healthcare & Social Safety Net

This bill enacts significant reforms across Medicaid, the ACA, SNAP, and student loans. The overarching themes are stricter eligibility, increased work and community engagement requirements, enhanced program integrity measures, and the introduction of specific social policy limitations.

Key Changes to Medicaid & SNAP

  • ✓
    Work/Community Engagement: Mandates that states require certain Medicaid recipients to complete 80 hours/month of work, community service, or education to maintain eligibility.
  • ✓
    Stricter SNAP Rules: Prevents administrative updates from increasing the cost of the Thrifty Food Plan (capping benefits), raises the work requirement age to 65, and cuts the federal share of administrative costs in half.
  • ✓
    Program Integrity: Implements new national systems to verify addresses and prevent duplicate enrollment in Medicaid across states. Tightens eligibility verification for both citizenship and income.
  • ✗
    Social Policy Restrictions: Prohibits federal funding (FFP) for gender transition procedures for minors under Medicaid/CHIP. Also defunds certain "prohibited entities" that provide abortion services from receiving Medicaid payments.

Education & ACA Reforms

  • ➔
    New Student Loan Plan: Forces most borrowers into a new, single "Repayment Assistance Plan" (RAP), eliminating other income-driven options and removing deferments for economic hardship or unemployment on new loans.
  • ➔
    Workforce Pell Grants: Creates a new "Workforce Pell Grant" for short-term vocational programs with strict performance metrics, while making regular Pell Grants harder to get for part-time students.
  • ➔
    Tighter ACA Enrollment: Shortens the open enrollment period and mandates stricter income and eligibility verification for individuals seeking subsidies on the ACA exchanges.
  • ✗
    EHB Limitation: Prohibits gender transition procedures from being included as an "Essential Health Benefit" that ACA-compliant plans must cover.

Energy & Environment Pivot

The bill engineers a sharp pivot in U.S. energy policy, moving away from support for renewable energy and towards maximizing domestic fossil fuel production. It mandates new oil, gas, and coal leasing while systematically rescinding funds and repealing regulations related to climate change and environmental protection.

Rescinded Climate & Environmental Funds (IRA & Others)

The bill rescinds billions in unobligated funds from programs established by the Inflation Reduction Act and other environmental laws. The chart below shows the breakdown of some major funding areas targeted for cuts.

Pro-Fossil Fuel Mandates

  • ▶
    Mandatory Leasing: Requires immediate resumption of quarterly onshore oil & gas lease sales in 9+ states and mandates new offshore sales.
  • ▶
    Alaska Drilling: Reinstates canceled leases and mandates new lease sales in the National Petroleum Reserve-Alaska and the Coastal Plain.
  • ▶
    Coal Expansion: Mandates expedited processing of coal leases and requires an additional 4 million acres to be made available for leasing.
  • ▶
    Streamlined Permitting: Introduces a "permit-by-rule" process for oil and gas drilling to speed up approvals.

Environmental Repeals

  • ▶
    Clean Air Act Repeals: Eliminates the Greenhouse Gas Reduction Fund, grants for clean heavy-duty vehicles, and programs to reduce port pollution.
  • ▶
    Vehicle Standards: Repeals EPA emissions standards and NHTSA fuel economy (CAFE) standards for new vehicles.
  • ▶
    Methane Program: Repeals and rescinds all funding for the methane emissions reduction incentive program for the oil and gas industry.
  • ▶
    New Renewable Fees: Introduces new acreage rent and capacity fees on wind and solar projects on federal lands.

Reshaping Governance & Federal Work

The bill fundamentally alters the balance of power between Congress and federal agencies, while also transforming the nature of federal employment. It introduces new hurdles for regulation and restructures retirement and job protections for civil servants.

Shift in Regulatory Power

New procedures are established to give Congress direct approval power over significant agency rules, slowing down the regulatory process.

Agency Proposes Rule

Agency must now provide exhaustive cost, job, and inflation impact reports.

NEW: Congressional Approval

Any "major rule" that increases revenue cannot take effect unless Congress passes a joint resolution of approval.

NEW: Annual Sunset Review

Agencies must submit 20% of existing rules for Congressional re-approval each year; they expire if not approved.

Rule Blocked or Expires

Failure to get Congressional approval prevents rule implementation or renewal.

Federal Employment Transformed

The bill overhauls the federal retirement system and introduces "at-will" employment, creating a two-tiered system for new hires.

  • ▲
    Increased FERS Contributions: Mandates significant, phased-in increases to retirement contributions for most federal employees, reducing take-home pay.
  • ▼
    "High-5" Pension Calculation: Changes the pension calculation from being based on the highest 3 years of salary to the highest 5, typically resulting in a lower pension.
  • ↔️
    At-Will Employment Option: New hires can choose "at-will" status (no appeal rights for removal) to avoid higher retirement contributions, fundamentally altering civil service protections.
